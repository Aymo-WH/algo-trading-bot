# Evidence Brief: Cross-Sectional Long-Short on a ~50-ETF Universe (Phase R, 2026-07-07)

Produced by a web-research subagent (R2b). Citations are to primary/practitioner
sources; grades are the subagent's strength-of-evidence assessment. Basis for the
signal selection in `research/design.md`.

## 1. Effective breadth — Grade: strong (against naive breadth)
The Fundamental Law counts *independent* bets, not tickers. Practitioner PCA work (ReSolve, "Tactical Alpha" series) shows the 30-stock Dow contained **1** statistically significant independent bet (Kaiser-Guttman + random-matrix correction), while a diversified global asset-class universe captures most available macro bets — on the order of **5-10 significant components**, with PC1 (global equity beta) typically absorbing 40-70% of variance across sector/country ETFs. Grinold's law is further degraded because bets are cross-sectionally correlated and serially persistent at weekly rebalance (Ding/JOIM, "Rethinking the Fundamental Law"). **Credit ~5-10 effective bets, not 50.** With IC ≈ 0.02-0.05 and breadth ~8×52 quasi-independent decisions/yr, gross Sharpe ~0.3-0.8 is the arithmetic ceiling for a single signal — matching realized results in Baz-Granger-Harvey et al. 2015, "Dissecting Investment Strategies" (single cross-sectional styles on macro universes: SR ~0.2-0.7; combined: ~1). Published ETF-specific cross-sectional work is thin — mostly Quantpedia-style sector-rotation backtests (SR ~0.4-0.7 gross, pre-decay) and ML papers (Poh-Zohren et al. 2020) with in-sample-flattering numbers.

## 2. Signal families at ETF/asset-class level
- **Cross-sectional momentum (3-12m), sectors/countries/asset classes** — *moderate*. Asness-Moskowitz-Pedersen 2013-era work found it everywhere (country equity SR ~0.4-0.5 standalone); but Baz et al. 2015 show macro momentum is **markedly stronger time-series than cross-sectional**, and McLean-Pontiff 2016 document 26% out-of-sample / 58% post-publication decay across anomalies — momentum among the most-arbitraged. Post-2010 sector/country momentum is roughly half its pre-publication Sharpe.
- **Short-term reversal (1w-1m)** — *weak-to-contradicted at index level*. The stock-level effect is largely microstructure (bid-ask bounce, liquidity provision — Dai-Medhat-Novy-Marx; Da et al.); aggregating to industry/index portfolios removes most of it, and what survives (intra-industry, overnight-intraday) requires stock-level granularity we don't have. At weekly ETF level after 5-10 bps this is very likely negative. **Skip.**
- **Low-vol / BAB across asset classes** — *moderate*. Frazzini-Pedersen 2014: BAB positive in every asset class, SR ~0.2-0.5 per class (0.78 in US stocks). Caveats: needs leverage of low-beta legs, and Novy-Marx/Velikov-style critiques trim it. Usable as a *defensive tilt*, standalone SR modest.
- **Carry** — *strong (best cross-asset evidence)*. Koijen-Moskowitz-Pedersen-Vrugt 2018: carry predicts returns in every asset class; per-class SR ~0.4-0.8, diversified global carry ~0.9-1.1 gross (pre-costs, futures-based). At ETF level carry proxies exist (yield curves, dividend/earnings yield, commodity futures slope via index roll) but are noisier than futures implementations.
- **Seasonality** — *moderate, underexploited*. Keloharju-Linnainmaa-Nyberg 2016: same-calendar-month relative-strength works in **country indices and commodities**, with near-zero correlation to other strategies (0.02, −0.09) — genuine diversifier; capacity small but fine at our size.
- **Lead-lag / cross-asset spillover** — *weak*. Documented mostly intra-equity at horizons arbitraged away post-2000s; little credible post-2010 ETF-level evidence net of costs. **Skip as a core signal.**

## 3. Realistic Sharpe — Grade: strong
AQR's stated planning number: one style in one asset class SR ~**0.2-0.3** net; a diversified multi-style, multi-asset book ~**0.7 net** (Understanding Style Premia / Alternative Thinking) — and QSPIX's live record undershot that. On ~5-10 effective bets with 3-4 signals, **0.5 net is plausible but not conservative; a backtest >1.0-1.2 net on this universe should be presumed leakage/overfit; >1.5 is a bug signal.** The mission's §3.3(f) bound is well supported.

## 4. Costs — Grade: strong
Tier 1 (SPY/QQQ/IWM/TLT/GLD): spreads <1 bp. Tier 2 liquid sector/country (XLE, EEM, EFA, EWZ): ~1-5 bps. Smaller country/commodity ETFs: 5-15 bps+. **5 bps/side is fair-to-conservative for the liquid half, mildly optimistic for the tail — 5-10 bps/side blended is the right baseline.** Weekly-rebalanced rank strategies on slow signals (12m momentum, carry, seasonality) turn over ~200-600% annualized one-way; at 7.5 bps that's ~15-45 bps/yr drag — costs are *not* the binding constraint; breadth is. Fast signals (weekly reversal) turn over 2000%+ and die on costs.

## 5. Alternative designs — Grade: moderate-to-strong for TS overlay; weak for vol-managed
- **Time-series momentum overlay: the strongest add.** Baz et al. 2015 and Moskowitz-Ooi-Pedersen 2012 / Babu et al. "Trends Everywhere" show TS-momentum beats XS-momentum on macro universes and diversifies it; Hurst-Ooi-Pedersen "A Century of Evidence" shows century-long robustness, though post-2010 trend was flat-to-weak (crowding debate).
- **Vol-managed (Moreira-Muir 2017): treat skeptically.** Cederburg-O'Doherty-Wang-Yan 2020: across 103 strategies, real-time implementable versions show **no systematic Sharpe improvement**. Use vol-*targeting* for risk control, not as an alpha source.
- **Regime-conditioning**: mostly in-sample storytelling; few pre-registered out-of-sample wins. Low prior.

## 6. Failure modes — Grade: strong
Momentum crashes (Daniel-Moskowitz 2016): −73% in 3 months (2009), occurring in panic states + market rebounds — dollar-neutral ≠ beta-neutral, since loser legs become high-beta after crashes. The 2018-2020 "quant winter" hit cross-sectional value/momentum hard (crowding + regime). Crisis correlation spikes collapse the ~5-10 effective bets toward ~1-2 exactly when leverage hurts. Mitigations with evidence: beta-hedge dynamically (not just dollar-neutral), vol-target, cap per-PC exposure.

## Bottom line (subagent's synthesis)
For a 50-ETF weekly book in 2026, best ex-ante families, in order: **(1) carry**, **(2) 3-12m momentum implemented primarily as time-series/trend with a cross-sectional tilt**, **(3) defensive/low-beta tilt**, **(4) calendar seasonality** (small but near-zero correlation — cheap diversification). **Skip:** short-term reversal and lead-lag spillovers. Apply a ~40-50% haircut to any backtested Sharpe per McLean-Pontiff, budget 5-10 bps/side, and size expectations off ~5-10 effective bets: **net 0.5 is an ambitious-but-defensible target; anything above ~1.2 in backtest should trigger a leakage audit, not celebration.**
